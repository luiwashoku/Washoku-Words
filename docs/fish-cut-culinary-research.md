# Fish-card culinary descriptions

Reviewed 2026-10-08. All 52 descriptions in `data/fish-cards.json` now replace the original location-only examples. They remain short, bilingual and suitable for spoken study. Names, card IDs, illustrations and existing quiz descriptions are preserved.

The notes distinguish anatomy, edible flesh, presentation, preparation and useful trimmings. Culinary recommendations are an editorial synthesis of the sources below, not universal rules for every species. In particular, thin sardine bones used for crackers do not imply that thick bones are edible; amadai scale frying is a species-specific technique. Bloodline muscle is distinguished from the blood residue washed out of the belly cavity. Upper/lower fillet names describe working orientation, not quality grades.

Terminology limits: exact standardized definitions for 背のだいみ and ヒレ上 were not established by the sources reviewed. Their descriptions therefore recommend confirming the actual section and grain with the supplier. The original labels and accepted name recordings remain unchanged. Numbered belly blocks are interpreted using the supplied deck's head/middle/tail order and cross-checked against the widely documented kami/naka/shimo pattern; fat grades are explicitly conditional.

Audio: replacement descriptions use reviewed hiragana and contextual particles, with complete phrase boundaries and Marin generation speed 1.0. Whole-sentence intonation is provisional under the user's standing permission. Lexical は is retained in はずします and は (刃), rather than changed by a global particle substitution. Existing primary-name recordings are reused. File decoding and non-silent signal checks are not listening or audible pitch verification.
